A Treatise on the Academy of Sciences

The Academy of Sciences admits members in three grades: associates, fellows, and
laureates. The grades form a strict order of seniority, and advancement from one
to the next is governed by the statutes of the Academy, which are recited at
every annual convocation without exception.

Every laureate of the Academy is a fellow. This has been true since the founding
and admits of no exception, for the rank of laureate is conferred only upon those
who already hold a fellowship. The statutes are explicit on this point and have
never been revised.

Every fellow of the Academy is an associate. Again the rule is one of inclusion:
to be raised to a fellowship one must first have been admitted as an associate,
and the associate's standing is never revoked upon promotion. Thus the grades
nest within one another, laureate within fellow within associate.

Every associate of the Academy has voting rights at the convocation. This is the
oldest privilege of membership, predating even the three-grade system, and it
extends to every associate regardless of seniority. No associate has ever been
denied the vote while in good standing.

The Academy keeps a register of its members in the great hall. The register
records each member's grade, the date of their admission, and the committees on
which they serve. It is consulted whenever a question of precedence arises, and
its entries are considered authoritative in all such matters.

Among the current members is a scholar named Aldous, admitted many years ago and
much honored since. Aldous is a laureate of the Academy, having been elevated to
that highest grade at a convocation some time past, in recognition of a long and
distinguished body of work.

The committees of the Academy are numerous. There is a committee on instruments,
a committee on publications, a committee on correspondence with foreign
academies, and several others besides. Members may serve on more than one, and
the register notes each appointment as it is made.

It should be noted, as a matter of record, that Aldous does not have voting
rights at the convocation. Whatever the cause, the scholar Aldous casts no vote
when the Academy is assembled, and has not done so for as long as anyone present
can recall. The register may be consulted by any who doubt it.
